About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Weierstraß Institute, working in the group of Prof. Michael Hintermüller. At heart, I'm drawn to problems where mathematics meets the real world: the stabilization of nonlinear acoustic waves, gas transport on pipeline networks, and generalized Nash equilibrium problems that model strategic interactions between competing agents. What connects this work is less a single topic than a way of thinking. I like to take a messy, real system, find the PDE or optimization structure hiding inside it, and build methods that are rigorous enough to trust and practical enough to use.

Before my current position, I was a PhD student at the University of Memphis, working under Irena Lasiecka and Roberto Triggiani, two mathematicians whose guidance shaped how I approach analysis to this day. My dissertation focused on singular perturbation problems and boundary stabilization of nonlinear acoustic waves, with applications to high intensity focused ultrasound procedures. That work gave me a solid foundation in the analysis, stabilization, and control of hyperbolic PDEs, a foundation I keep building on.

That same curiosity has pulled me into some unexpected places too: epidemic modeling and non-compliance dynamics, quantum optimal control, hydrogen embrittlement in gas pipelines, and even machine learning for voice spoofing detection. I like collaborations that start from a concrete question rather than a fixed toolkit, and I'm always happy to hear from people in academia or industry working on problems where PDEs, optimization, or game theory might help, even if the connection isn't obvious yet.

Lately I've been especially interested in bringing this mindset to data science and quantitative finance: stochastic calculus, optimization under uncertainty, and computational modeling applied to markets rather than materials or fluids.
